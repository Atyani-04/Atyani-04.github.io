Automated 3D scanning and illumination rig

My final-year individual project at the University of Nottingham. The rig automates image capture from multiple angles under controlled, even lighting, producing the image sets needed for 3D reconstruction by photogrammetry. The target application is surgical visualisation.

Role
Solo: requirements, firmware, electronics, mechanical design and validation
Year
2025 to 2026
Budget
£200 cap, finished 16% under
Outcome
First Class dissertation, 61 pages
Tools
ESP32 (C++), Raspberry Pi 5, two Pi Camera Module 3, picamera2, Python, OpenCV, KiCad, Fusion 360, OpenSCAD, ReCap Photo, CloudCompare

What I built

  • Firmware. ESP32 firmware in C++ for real-time control of the LEDs and the stepper motor, integrated with a dual-camera capture pipeline on a Raspberry Pi 5 running Linux. The two cameras are synchronised with picamera2.
  • Electronics. A custom LED ring PCB designed in KiCad, from schematic capture through layout. I hand-soldered the SMD and through-hole parts, then brought the board up and debugged it with a bench supply, function generator and oscilloscope.
  • Mechanics. Gears and mounts designed in Fusion 360 and OpenSCAD, including a double-helical ring gear driven by a NEMA 17 stepper through an A4988 driver. The whole assembly was 3D printed.
  • Validation. An automated Python and OpenCV harness that measures image overlap and illumination uniformity, plus photogrammetry in ReCap Photo and cloud-to-mesh comparison in CloudCompare.
  • Documentation. Test criteria derived from a 9-point requirements specification, with design decisions and trade-offs written up in a 61-page dissertation.

What went wrong

I ran into a motor driver fault with the original L298N. I tracked it down through systematic board-level fault finding and fixed it by switching to an A4988 driver.

Partway through the build, the university 3D printer broke and was out of action for two weeks, and outsourcing the prints was too expensive. I taught myself to design for laser-cut acrylic and redesigned the base in a single day to stay on schedule. The project still received a First.
